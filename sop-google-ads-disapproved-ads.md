# SOP: Resolving Disapproved Ads in Google Ads

> **Sample document.** This SOP is written for a fictional company ("Northwind Outdoor Co.") to demonstrate SOP structure, ownership, decision points, and governance. It uses publicly known Google Ads concepts and is not based on any employer's internal content.

## 1. Purpose

Make sure disapproved ads are found, fixed, and verified quickly and the same way every time, so campaigns lose as little delivery as possible and every decision is traceable.

## 2. Scope

**In scope:** Ads and assets with a status of *Disapproved* or *Limited by policy* in any active campaign.

**Out of scope:** Account-level suspensions, billing problems, and policy questions about new product launches. Escalate these using Section 8.

## 3. Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Campaign Specialist** | Checks for disapprovals, applies fixes, logs the outcome |
| **Paid Search Lead** | Approves appeals and policy-sensitive copy changes, owns this SOP |
| **Legal/Compliance Reviewer** | Reviews claims in ad copy when the policy topic involves them (for example, health, pricing, or comparative claims) |
| **Account Manager** | Informs the client or business owner about delivery impact |

## 4. Before you start

You need:

- Standard or higher access to the Google Ads account
- Access to the team's Disapproval Log (shared spreadsheet)
- The current Brand Copy Guidelines

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Find disapproved ads

1. Sign in to the Google Ads account.
2. Open **Policy manager** from the Tools menu and review ads and assets with a policy status.
3. For each item, note the campaign, ad group, ad or asset, and the policy topic shown.
4. Add a row for each item to the Disapproval Log with today's date and your name.

**Check:** Every disapproved item in the account has a row in the log.

### 5.2 Diagnose the cause

1. Open the item's policy details and read the stated policy topic and reason.
2. Read the linked Google Ads policy page for that topic.
3. Compare the ad text, assets, and landing page against the policy. Check the landing page as well as the ad, because many disapprovals come from the page.
4. Record the likely cause in the log (for example, *destination not working*, *misleading claim*, *trademark*, *punctuation or capitalization*).

**Check:** The log states a specific cause, not just the policy topic name.

### 5.3 Choose a path

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| The ad or asset clearly violates the policy | **Fix** it (5.4) |
| The landing page is the problem | **Fix the page** with the web team, then resubmit (5.4) |
| The cause is unclear, or the ad appears compliant | **Appeal** (5.5) after Paid Search Lead approval |
| The policy topic involves legal or regulated claims | Send to the **Legal/Compliance Reviewer** before any change |
| The item needs a policy exception, certification, or a new feature | **Escalate** (Section 8) |

### 5.4 Fix and resubmit

1. Edit the ad or asset to remove the issue. Keep the change as small as possible.
2. Follow the Brand Copy Guidelines. Do not add claims you cannot support.
3. Save the edit. Google Ads re-reviews edited ads automatically.
4. In the log, record what you changed and the time you resubmitted.

**Check:** The ad shows a review or approved status after saving. Most reviews finish within one business day, but allow longer if the item is still pending.

### 5.5 Appeal a decision

Only appeal when you believe the ad follows policy.

1. Get approval from the Paid Search Lead and note it in the log.
2. Start the appeal from the item's policy details in Policy manager.
3. Provide a short, factual explanation if the form asks for one. Do not argue or repeat the ad text.
4. Record the appeal date and expected response time in the log.
5. Do not edit the item while the appeal is pending, because an edit can restart the review.

### 5.6 Verify the result

1. Return to the item within one business day of resubmitting or appealing.
2. Confirm the status is now *Approved* or *Eligible*.
3. If the item is still disapproved after two fix attempts, escalate (Section 8).
4. Update the log with the final status and close the row.

## 6. Quality checks

- The log has no open rows older than 3 business days without a note explaining why.
- Every appeal has a recorded approval.
- The Paid Search Lead reviews the log weekly for repeated causes. A cause that appears three or more times becomes an update to the Brand Copy Guidelines.

## 7. Records

| Record | Location | Retention |
|---|---|---|
| Disapproval Log | Team shared drive | 24 months |
| Appeal approvals | Disapproval Log (approval column) | 24 months |
| Copy changes | Version history in the shared ad-copy sheet | 24 months |

## 8. Escalation

Escalate to the Paid Search Lead when:

- An item is still disapproved after two fix attempts
- A large share of an account's ads are disapproved at once
- You see a warning about account-level policy issues

Include: the account name, a link to the item, the policy topic, what you tried, and the log row number. The Lead decides whether to contact Google Ads support or involve Legal.

## 9. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Ad keeps getting disapproved after edits | The landing page, not the ad, is the issue | Review the page against the same policy and coordinate a fix with the web team |
| No resubmit option appears | The item is still under review | Wait for the review to finish before taking action |
| Status doesn't change after a day | Review is delayed | Re-check the next business day, then escalate if there is still no change |
